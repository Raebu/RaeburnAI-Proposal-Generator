# AI Governance, Risk & Compliance Standard

**Owner:** Group Governance / Security / Data Protection
**Effective:** 26 August 2026
**Review:** at least annually and after material change

## Governance programme
Raeburn maintains a leadership-sponsored governance programme for information security, privacy and AI. The programme covers policies, risk management, secure engineering, suppliers, data governance, continuity, incident response, assurance and customer transparency. Material risks are escalated to accountable leadership.

## Roles and responsibility
- Executive sponsor: accountable for risk acceptance and strategic oversight.
- Security owner: technical/security policy, assurance and incident governance.
- DPO/data-protection owner: privacy, DPIAs, rights requests and regulatory privacy matters.
- Product/engineering owner: implementation, testing, release evidence and remediation.
- AI Ethics & Impact Committee: multidisciplinary review of material AI risks, fairness, human oversight and explainability.

## AI risk-management programme
Every material AI service has an AI risk record covering intended use, prohibited use, data, model/provider, security, privacy, misuse, hallucination, bias/fairness, explainability, human oversight, resilience, supplier and regulatory risks. Risks are rated, assigned, treated and reviewed before material release and at least annually.

## Policy review
Security, privacy and AI policies are reviewed annually and when material changes occur. Owners record effective date, review date, exceptions and change history.

## Exception process
Policy exceptions require a documented business rationale, affected control, risk assessment, compensating control, owner, approver and expiry date. High-risk exceptions require executive/security approval. Expired exceptions are invalid until reapproved.

## Information-security programme
The programme spans governance, IAM, cryptography, secure SDLC, vulnerability management, logging, incident response, business continuity, supplier assurance, data lifecycle, network/web security and AI security.

## Regulatory mapping
The compliance register identifies applicable obligations, including UK GDPR/Data Protection Act 2018, PECR where applicable, contractual/customer requirements, intellectual-property/confidentiality obligations, relevant AI regulation/principles in markets served, and voluntary assurance frameworks such as CSA AICM/AI-CAIQ. Applicability is reviewed at least annually and before entry into materially new jurisdictions/use cases.

## External knowledge and special-interest contact
Security and AI governance owners maintain contact with relevant professional/security communities, standards bodies and public guidance sources, including Cloud Security Alliance materials used for this programme. Material guidance is evaluated for applicability and translated into backlog/control updates.

## Acceptable use
AI services must not be used to facilitate unlawful activity, credential theft, malware, unauthorised access, discriminatory decision-making without appropriate safeguards, deceptive impersonation, or processing of data beyond contractual/legal authority. Users remain responsible for validating generated content before material business reliance. Violations may result in access restriction or termination.

## Continuous effectiveness
Acceptable-use and AI controls are evaluated through security reviews, incident/abuse reports, support feedback, model/provider changes and periodic impact/risk assessments. Control changes are versioned and traceable.

## AI impact assessment
Material AI launches/changes require an impact assessment covering: purpose and affected parties; legal/privacy basis; data sensitivity; model/provider; security threats; misuse; human oversight; explainability; bias/fairness; accessibility; customer impact; retention; supplier risk; and residual risk. High unresolved risk blocks release unless formally accepted at the correct authority level.

## Bias and fairness
Risk-based fairness evaluation is required where outputs could materially affect people or differential treatment. Test cases must cover plausible protected/near-protected attributes without unnecessarily collecting sensitive data. Findings, limitations and mitigations are documented. The Proposal Generator is advisory and not approved as an autonomous employment, credit, insurance, healthcare or legal-rights decision maker.

## AI Ethics & Impact Committee
A standing committee is established by role: executive sponsor/chair, security representative, data-protection representative, product/engineering representative and an independent/challenge participant where proportionate. Quorum is three including security or data protection. The committee reviews high-impact AI use cases, material exceptions, unresolved fairness/privacy issues and major AI incidents. Decisions and actions are recorded.

## Explainability requirement
Required explainability is proportionate to risk. For generative consulting outputs, explainability means users can understand the intended purpose, source context supplied, material assumptions/limitations, deterministic calculations where used, provider/model identity where appropriate, and the need for human validation. Raeburn does not claim interpretability of a third-party foundation model's internal neural reasoning.

## Explainability evaluation
Each material AI service documents what can and cannot be explained, how assumptions and limitations are surfaced, and where human review is mandatory. Higher-impact use cases require stronger traceability and justification before approval.

## Evidence
Evidence includes governance policies, risk register, regulatory mapping, exception register, committee charter/minutes, impact/DPIA records, acceptable-use policy, fairness assessment, explainability assessment, incidents/findings and remediation records.
